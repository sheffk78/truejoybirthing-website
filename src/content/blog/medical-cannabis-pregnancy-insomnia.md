## The Reality of Pregnancy Insomnia

Pregnancy insomnia is incredibly common, but that does not make it any easier to handle. As your body grows and changes, sleep becomes a challenge. You might be dealing with round ligament pain, heartburn, or a baby who thinks your bladder is a trampoline. Add in the hormonal shifts and the natural anxiety of preparing for a new baby, and it is no wonder you are awake.

With my third baby, I remember feeling so frustrated because I knew I needed to sleep to build a healthy baby, but my body just would not cooperate. I tried every trick in the book. Sometimes they worked, and sometimes I just had to accept that I was going to have a rough night. It is completely normal to feel desperate for a solution when you are running on empty.

When we are desperate, we often look for quick fixes. You might see headlines about natural remedies and wonder if they are safe for pregnancy. It is so important to look closely at these remedies before trying them, especially when you are growing a little one.

**What this means for you:** Your exhaustion is real, and your desire to find a solution is completely valid. Give yourself grace when you have a bad night, and remember that not every popular sleep remedy is right for pregnancy.

## What the Research Says About Medical Cannabis

A new Cochrane systematic review looked into the use of medical cannabis for insomnia. Cochrane reviews are considered the gold standard in medical research because they gather all the available data on a topic and analyze it together. The researchers wanted to know if medical cannabis actually improves sleep or daytime functioning in people with insomnia.

The review found that the existing evidence is too uncertain to determine whether medical cannabis helps with sleep at all. Evidence indicates that there is a critical lack of definitive clinical data to support its use for this common complaint. The researchers found that we simply do not have enough reliable information to say it works, and we certainly do not have enough information to say it is safe for pregnant women and their developing babies.

When research suggests a treatment lacks evidence, it means we need to be cautious. Just because something is natural or derived from a plant does not automatically mean it is safe during pregnancy. Without solid clinical data, we do not know how it might affect fetal development, pregnancy outcomes, or your own daytime functioning. The review highlights that we need much more rigorous research before this can be considered a viable option.

**What this means for you:** Do not assume that a natural label equals a safe or effective label. Because the evidence is so uncertain, it is best to hold off on medical cannabis during pregnancy and focus on methods we know are safe.

## Safe, Proven Sleep Habits for Expecting Moms

Since the research does not support medical cannabis for pregnancy insomnia, what can you do? Try chatting with your care team about safe, proven sleep habits and routines to help you get some much-needed rest. There are several simple, non-medical strategies that can make a big difference in how you sleep.

First, look at your sleep environment. Is your room cool, dark, and quiet? Using a body pillow or a pregnancy pillow can take the pressure off your hips and lower back, making it much easier to get comfortable on your side. I always recommend a white noise machine to block out household sounds that might wake you up.

Second, establish a wind-down routine. Just like we do for toddlers, our bodies need a signal that it is time to sleep. Try taking a warm bath, drinking a cup of caffeine-free herbal tea (check with your provider first), and reading a physical book instead of scrolling on your phone. The blue light from screens can interfere with your natural melatonin production.

Finally, practice relaxation breathing. When your mind is racing about the upcoming birth, slow, deep breaths can calm your nervous system. If you are planning out your birth preferences, getting your thoughts on paper can also relieve mental clutter. You can use our [Free Joyful Birth Plan template](/birth-plan-template/) to organize your wishes so you do not have to think about them at 3:00 AM.

**What this means for you:** Build a simple, screen-free wind-down routine tonight. Focus on physical comfort with pillows and a cool room to give your body the best chance at rest.

## Making Peace with Imperfect Sleep

At the end of the day, pregnancy sleep is rarely perfect. With my seventh baby, I finally accepted that some nights were just going to be rough. Instead of fighting it and getting frustrated, I learned to rest. If I woke up and could not go back to sleep, I would move to the couch, put on a gentle podcast, and just let my body rest. 

Resting is not the same as sleeping, but it is still valuable. Giving your body a break, even if you are awake, allows your muscles to relax and takes the pressure off. The research on medical cannabis suggests it is not the answer we are looking for, but that does not mean you are out of options. Combine safe sleep habits with a supportive care team, and give yourself permission to have an imperfect night.

**What this means for you:** Rest when you can, even if it is not deep sleep. Accept this season for what it is, and trust that your body is doing important work, even when you are awake.

## Questions to Bring to Your Care Team

When you head to your next prenatal appointment, bring these questions along to help guide your conversation about sleep:

- What safe, over-the-counter sleep aids can I use during pregnancy?
- Can you recommend specific relaxation techniques or therapies for pregnancy insomnia?
- Are there any underlying physical issues, like heartburn or restless leg syndrome, that we need to address?
- How can I safely adjust my sleep position as my belly grows?
- What signs of sleep deprivation should prompt me to call the office?
